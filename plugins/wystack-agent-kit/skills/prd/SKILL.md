# PRD

Use the user's template, then the project's existing convention. With neither, choose the smallest structure that makes the product decision implementable.

A PRD owns product intent: affected users, the problem, outcomes, scope and non-goals, constraints, success signals, and the requirements or stories that define acceptance. Keep architecture and implementation choices in the technical spec.

Separate evidence from assumptions. Resolve ambiguity that would change scope or acceptance; leave harmless detail to implementation. Prefer observable behavior over feature inventory.

Complete when design and delivery can proceed without guessing who the work serves, what outcome matters, or what is outside the boundary.
