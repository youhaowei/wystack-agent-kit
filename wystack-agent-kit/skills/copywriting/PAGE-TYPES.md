# Page-Type Templates

Per-page-type structure. Same six-principle baseline; different emphasis per type.

## Homepage

**Audience**: mixed — first-time visitors, varied intents. Lead with the broadest value prop, then route to specific pages; don't try to close the deal here.

1. Hero (headline, subheadline, primary CTA, visual)
2. Logos / social proof bar
3. Three-pillar benefits (or how-it-works)
4. Use-case showcase (different audience paths)
5. Deeper feature section
6. Testimonials / case studies
7. Pricing teaser → link to pricing page
8. FAQ
9. Final CTA

Watch for: hero copy so vague it pleases everyone; no clear next step for non-converters.

## Landing page (campaign-specific)

**Audience**: single, defined — from a known source (ad, email, partner). Single message, single CTA, headline matched to the source's promise, argument completed on one page.

1. Hero matched to the traffic source's promise
2. Problem framing (pain the visitor feels)
3. Solution (positioned for this specific audience)
4. Social proof (specific to this segment)
5. How it works
6. Risk reversal (guarantee, trial, no-credit-card)
7. CTA (matching the hero CTA)

Watch for: competing CTAs; headline/source mismatch (instant bounce); copy generic enough to sit on any landing page.

## Pricing page

**Audience**: evaluating fit and cost. Answer "which is right for me?" and make the recommended plan obvious — they're past the pitch.

1. Brief value-prop reminder (not the whole pitch)
2. Pricing tiers (usually 3) with monthly/annual toggle
3. Most-popular tier visually emphasized
4. Per tier: price, headline benefit, included features, CTA
5. Feature comparison table
6. FAQ (billing, cancellation, refunds, plan changes)
7. Enterprise / custom CTA
8. Trust signals (paying-customer logos, security badges)

**Pricing patterns** (see marketing-psychology reference): charm vs round pricing (charm for value, round for premium), decoy tier, anchoring (high tier first), mental-accounting reframes ("$1/day" vs "$30/month").

Watch for: "Contact sales" for everything; pricing walled off (kills self-serve); no visually recommended tier; tier names that don't signal fit ("Pro" / "Premium" / "Ultra" — for whom?).

## Feature page

**Audience**: evaluating a specific capability. Connect feature → benefit → outcome, with use cases and a clear path to try or buy.

1. Hero (the feature, what it solves)
2. Quick demo or screenshot
3. Feature breakdown (sub-capabilities)
4. Use-case scenarios (1–3 with specific personas)
5. Code/UI examples if technical
6. Customer quote about this feature
7. Related features (cross-link)
8. CTA (try it, see pricing, talk to sales)

Watch for: sub-capabilities listed without context; no use cases (reader can't picture using it); CTA mismatched to intent.

## About page

**Audience**: checking credibility and trust. Tell why you exist, connect mission to customer benefit — and still include a CTA.

1. Hero — what you do, framed as why it matters
2. Founding story (problem you experienced)
3. Mission / values (specific principles, no platitudes)
4. Team (photos, names, brief bios — not just leadership)
5. Customers / impact (numbers, named customers, outcomes)
6. Press / awards (if relevant)
7. Hiring CTA + product CTA

Watch for: company history with no customer connection; mission-statement filler; no faces (kills trust); no CTA (about-page visitors do convert).

## Comparison / vs page

**Audience**: directly comparing you to a competitor. Be honest — acknowledge competitor strengths, be explicit about who each is best for. These pages need real, citable facts (pricing, features, claims), not guesses.

Four formats by search intent (alternative · alternatives · you-vs-competitor · competitor-vs-competitor) with full structure and SEO guidance: [COMPARISON-PAGES.md](COMPARISON-PAGES.md).

## Blog post / long-form

**Audience**: topical interest via search or social. Deliver genuine value first; earn the right to pitch.

1. Compelling title (specific, promises a payoff)
2. Lede (hook + what the reader gets)
3. Body (scannable, sub-headings, code blocks if technical)
4. Examples and visuals
5. TL;DR or summary
6. Soft CTA (relevant product mention, related content, signup)

Watch for: buried lede; wall of text without sub-headings; aggressive pitch mid-content; no CTA at all.
